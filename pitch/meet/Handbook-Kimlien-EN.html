3. How a website works, explained simply

Think of a website as a house. There are three parts: the address (domain), the house (hosting), and the signpost (DNS) that sends visitors who type the address to the right house. Email is a separate mailbox, not inside the house, so it's not affected.

When we switch, we build the new house and change the signpost. The address stays the same, so customers notice no break.

7. Information to collect (tick as you go)

You don't need everything today. Just ask, and note when they'll send it.

  • Domain: who holds the account for phanscustomtailor.com and tailormadesuit.net; which one as main (we suggest phanscustomtailor.com).
  • Photos: the villa, the two of them, finished garments, cloth; the original logo. Ask when they can send them.
  • Missing prices: blazer, waistcoat, overcoat, women's. VND only, or USD too?
  • Details: the year they started (old site says “since 2013”), Tết closures, whether customers abroad can reorder. Turnaround: the old site says 5 working days but their Koalendar says 7–10 days. Ask which is right.
  • Someone at Phan's to read the Vietnamese.
  • Accounts: the Koalendar link, the official Facebook page, Google Business access.
  • Main contact person and channel (Zalo, WhatsApp, email).
  • 3D team: do they want us to coordinate? Get a contact.

8. Don't promise

  • A specific price, a discount or payment terms.
  • Online sales or card payment in the first phase.
  • The AI fitting room.
  • A specific launch date.
  • A dashboard for them to edit the site themselves.
  • Buying a new domain (Phan's already has one).
