1. How a website works

Think of a website as a house. There are three parts: the address (domain), the house (hosting), and the signpost (DNS) that sends visitors who type the address to the right house. Email is a separate mailbox, not inside the house, so it's not affected.

When we switch, we build the new house and change the signpost. The address stays the same, so customers notice no break.

4. Showing the mock-up

  • Open the link and tap MENU (top left) to see the pages: Suits & pricing, Process, Fabrics, Shop, Contact.
  • Tap EN next to the booking button to switch to VI or 中文. You stay on the same page.
  • Hand them the phone to scroll themselves; most customers look on a phone.
  • On the Contact page, scroll to “Choose a time for your consultation”: Phan's Koalendar calendar shows right inside the site, like the old one. Tap “Book now” to show the free times, but don't finish a booking: it would create a real appointment.
  • Don't test the enquiry form on the Contact page: it sends a real email to Phan's.
  • The “Try-On” buttons (AI try-on) on product photos: we're not offering this yet. If they tap or ask, see the answer in section 3.

5. Technical details to ask for

  • Domain: who holds the account for phanscustomtailor.com and tailormadesuit.net (so they can invite us), and which one as main. We suggest phanscustomtailor.com.
  • Koalendar: who manages the calendar. It says 7–10 days for a suit, the old site says 5 working days: ask which is right.
  • Google Business (Phan's page on Google Maps): who can manage it, so the new site can be linked.
  • Facebook: is the official page facebook.com/phanstailor or /phantailor?
  • 3D team: if they want us to coordinate, get their technical contact.

6. Don't promise

  • Online sales or card payment in the first phase.
  • The AI fitting room.
  • A specific launch date.
  • A dashboard for them to edit the site themselves.
  • Buying a new domain (Phan's already has one).
